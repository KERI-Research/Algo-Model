# AI-Driven Discovery of Metabolic Biomarkers Linking Diabetes and Pancreatic Cancer for Early Detection

## Summary

The project aims to develop a new approach to early cancer detection by uncovering hidden metabolic signals linking diabetes and pancreatic cancer. Rather than relying on single biomarkers, the project explores whether combinations of clinical, biochemical and behavioural data can reveal early warning patterns. The key questions are: can subtle changes in metabolic trajectories signal the presence of early pancreatic cancer? Can these signals distinguish high-risk individuals within the large population of patients with diabetes? And can these insights be translated into practical risk stratification tools?

## Research Context

Pancreatic cancer is still largely diagnosed too late for effective treatment. While diabetes is common and routinely monitored, creating an opportunity to identify high-risk individuals earlier if meaningful signals can be extracted. Although the link between diabetes and pancreatic cancer is well recognised, it remains under-exploited from an innovation perspective. Current approaches focus on isolated risk factors, whereas the real signal is likely to be distributed across multiple variables and evolving over time. This project addresses that gap by treating the problem as a data integration and pattern discovery challenge, opening the door to a new generation of data-driven diagnostics.

## Variables to look for in the data

Some of the factors to search for:

- Type of diabetes
  - e.g. Type 1, Type 2, gestational
- Timing of diabetes onset
  - e.g. recent vs long-standing
- Duration of diabetes
  - e.g. years since diagnosis
- HbA1c levels
  - e.g. average, trends over time
- Blood biomarkers
  - e.g. C-peptide and insulin levels, CA 19-9
- Patients related profile
  - e.g. age, sex, weight loss, obesity
